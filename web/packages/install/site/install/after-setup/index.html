After setup on a NAS

Maktaba runs. This is what to look at next.

All NAS guides · العربية

Settings → This machine shows what Maktaba found: your NAS, its tier and memory, the video hardware, and the network.

Your libraries

The folders you gave setup are the ones Maktaba offers you; the libraries themselves are made in Maktaba. Sign in with the admin account, then Manage → Manage libraries → Add library, once for each folder (films, series, recordings). On a Light or Standard NAS a new library's first scan waits for the quiet hours (below): Run now on the library's page starts it at once.

Permissions

Each library shows Read and write, Read only or No access. A read-only library works fully for watching and searching; Maktaba keeps its subtitles in its database and leaves your files untouched, and only the writing features (converted copies, subtitles beside the videos) are off there. To change it, give the user Maktaba runs as the access you want in your NAS's settings (the library page says where), then press Check access.

Quiet hours

On a Light or Standard NAS, heavy work (a new library's first scan, speech to text, thumbnails, conversions) waits for the quiet hours, 01:00 to 06:00 by default, so the NAS stays quick for its own files during the day. Change them in Settings → This machine. A waiting job shows when it will run, and Run now starts it at once.

Speech to text

Maktaba measures how fast this NAS transcribes and shows it in Settings → This machine, under Speech to text, with the time its waiting audio will take. On a Light NAS it is off until you turn it on. Smaller models are faster but make more mistakes, especially in Arabic. When the wait is long, a computer with a GPU can join the home and do the work (below).

Hardware video

With an Intel or AMD chip, Settings → This machine says Video: hardware and the formats it converts. It needs the render device /dev/dri passed to the container, and the device's group added to it: stat -c %g /dev/dri/renderD128 on the NAS prints the number, which goes under group_add in the compose file (Extra Parameters' --group-add on Unraid). Without it Maktaba converts in software, and players play files as they are whenever they can.

Reverse proxy and firewall

Maktaba needs no reverse proxy: its own HTTPS name works on a NAS as anywhere. If you put the NAS's proxy in front anyway, pass the whole site to port 8088 with WebSocket on, no buffering, and no upload limit. A proxy at another address goes in Settings → Storage & discovery → Trusted proxies. If the NAS has a firewall, allow TCP 8088, 8080 and 8081 and UDP 5353 from your home network.

Updates

Maktaba updates itself inside its update window (Manage → Updates), each release checked before it is installed, and goes back by itself if a release cannot start. When your NAS's app catalog offers a newer image, applying it is safe too: the newer of the two runs, and an older image never replaces what Maktaba already runs.

Adding a computer with a GPU

A NAS is a good home for the library, but a computer with a graphics card transcribes many times faster. Install Maktaba on that computer and choose to join this home; then, in Manage → Machines, approve it under Asking to join with the code it shows. It takes the heavy work and reads your media from the NAS's shares. A Light NAS should stay the home or the storage, not join another home as a machine.
